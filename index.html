About Me · System Ideology & Practice

Interdependent Systems: Flow, Ergonomics & Tactile Feedback.

I view software through the lens of interdependent systems: the physical tolerances of an engine, the throughput of a supply chain, and the cognitive load on a human mind.

With a master’s in logistics and a foundation in market behavior, my work begins by observing flow, bottlenecks, and the unwritten patterns of daily operations. But mechanical efficiency means nothing if the interaction feels hostile. Guided by psychology, design fundamentals, and practical philosophy, I care deeply about how an interface feels in human hands—its cadence, clarity, and tactile feedback.

I spend my time building working prototypes and architectural case studies. My goal is to create responsive, quiet tools that respect human attention, absorb operational friction, and give genuine value back to the community.

01. PHYSICAL TOLERANCES

Engine Mechanics & Supply Chains

Software cannot operate in theoretical isolation. Grounded in a master's in logistics, every architecture starts by mapping raw operational throughput, bottlenecks, physical hardware bounds, and offline edge conditions.

Applied in: Ward 4B BLE Telemetry, PostGIS Triage
02. COGNITIVE ERGONOMICS

Cadence, Clarity & Tactile Feel

Mechanical throughput is futile if the experience feels hostile. Informed by psychology and practical philosophy, interfaces must possess calm cadence, zero fluff, immediate tactile feedback, and respect for human cognitive bandwidth.

Applied in: WCAG 2.2 AAA, AudioWorklet DAW, iTHINK
03. WORKING PROTOTYPES

Absorbing Friction & Returning Value

Ideas prove themselves in code that works. I build working prototypes that absorb friction from messy real-world systems and give genuine, verifiable utility back to clinicians, crews, and communities.

Applied in: The Observer, Civic Infrastructure Triage
